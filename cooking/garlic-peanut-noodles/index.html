    Garlic Peanut Noodles

    Source: Rainbow Plant Life

    Servings: 4

    Ingredients

    • 4 scallions
    • 20 ounces fresh udon noodles (or 8 ounces dried udon)
    • 4 cloves garlic
    • 1 1/2 tablespoons neutral oil
    • 2 tablespoons Chinese light soy sauce (or regular soy sauce or tamari)
    • 1 generous tablespoon rice vinegar
    • 2 teaspoons brown sugar (or cane sugar)
    • 1/4 teaspoon ground white pepper
    • Heaping 1/3 cup creamy peanut butter
    • 3 tablespoons to 1/3 cup hot water
    • 1/2 cup roasted peanuts (or cashews), roughly chopped
    • 1 red bell pepper, thinly sliced, or 8 ounces Persian cucumber, julienned

    Instructions

    1. Bring a large saucepan of water to a boil for the noodles.

    2. While the water heats, trim the scallions and slice them thinly on a bias. Soak in cold water for about 10 minutes to mellow the raw onion bite, then drain and pat dry. Grate or finely mince the garlic into a small bowl.

    3. Cook the noodles according to the package. Drain, rinse under cold water until they come down to room temperature, and transfer to a large bowl.

    4. Heat the neutral oil in your smallest saucepan over medium-high heat for at least 3 minutes, until very hot (350°F).

    5. Pour the hot oil over the garlic and let it sizzle. Rest 1 minute, then stir in the soy sauce, rice vinegar, sugar, and white pepper.

    6. Measure the hot water: 3 tablespoons for fresh udon, 1/3 cup for dried. Whisk the peanut butter with the hot water in a medium bowl until loosened, then whisk in the soy sauce and garlic mixture until smooth.

    7. Pour the peanut sauce over the noodles and toss well with tongs. If it is too thick, add another 1 to 2 tablespoons hot water. Stir in the drained scallions, chopped peanuts, and the bell pepper or cucumber. Taste and adjust with more soy sauce or rice vinegar. Fold in baked tofu if using.

    8. Top each serving with chili crisp.

    Notes

    Noodle amounts by type: 20 ounces fresh udon, 8 ounces dried udon, 28 to 30 ounces frozen udon, 10 to 12 ounces other fresh noodles, or thick rice noodles to keep it gluten-free.

    Fresh udon holds more moisture and needs less water in the sauce; dried noodles absorb more, so start with the larger amount. Peanut sauce thickens the moment it hits the noodles, so keep hot water nearby and loosen it as you toss.
